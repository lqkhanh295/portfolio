Entry 03 · Engineering Philosophy

How I Think & Build

A perspective shaped by testing enterprise applications in QA, writing disciplined database queries, and respecting clear layer boundaries.

01

Observe & Deconstruct

Before writing a single line of backend logic, I spend time understanding the actual business domain, edge conditions, and data lifecycles. Requirements are never just endpoints; they are contracts between people, systems, and time.

02

Structure with Clean Boundaries

I favor Clean Architecture principles because they keep business logic organized and decoupled from database drivers or external libraries. This makes code easier to test, refactor, and maintain.

03

Respect Data Consistency & Edge Cases

Real systems encounter unexpected inputs, simultaneous writes, and failed transactions. I rely on proper database transactions, input validation, and state handling to ensure business data stays consistent and dependable.

04

Verify from the Outside In

My internship as a Software Tester at FPT Software taught me that green unit tests can still harbor unexpected edge-case defects and payload mismatches. I verify by inspecting direct database states with T-SQL, testing edge cases with Postman, and thinking like a skeptical user.
